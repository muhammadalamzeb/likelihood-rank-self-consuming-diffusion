Self-consuming training loops—retraining generative models on their own outputs—can induce model collapse, including loss of rare modes. Prior work notes that bias toward high-quality synthetic samples worsens the quality–diversity tradeoff, and that task-level verification can stabilize labeled synthetic data. We study the most common unlabeled selection score for diffusion models: a denoising ELBO / likelihood proxy. On imbalanced Gaussian mixtures with a tiny DDPM, under matched real-data mixing and matched synthetic budget, first-generation minority-mass retention obeys bottom-k > random-k > top-k (means 0.57/0.36/0.17 over eight seed–imbalance pairs; order holds on 5/5 seeds at imbalance ρ=5). Paired Wilcoxon tests vs. random-k give p≈0.042 for both top and bottom contrasts. Likelihood top-k also concentrates majority mass relative to random-k. A natural “false stability” hypothesis—that top-k improves sliced W₂ while minorities die—is falsified. The ordering transfers to 8D GMM and to Digits in PCA space with nearest class-mean occupancy (3/3 seeds each). We release configs, seeds, logs, and analysis code.

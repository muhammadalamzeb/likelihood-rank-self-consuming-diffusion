Self-consuming training loops—retraining generative models on their own outputs—can induce model collapse, including loss of rare modes. We study unlabeled selection of synthetic samples by a denoising likelihood proxy in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures. Under matched real-mix ratio and matched synthetic budget, the first-generation minority-mass ratio r=m(1)/m(0) obeys bottom-k > random-k > top-k on a primary matrix of eight seed–imbalance cells (seeds {0,1,2} at ρ∈{5,10} plus seeds {3,4} at ρ=5 only; means 0.57/0.36/0.17). Paired contrasts vs random-k show large effects (Cohen dz≈1.0) with Wilcoxon p≈0.042 (Holm-adjusted p≈0.085); we treat the ordering as supported by effect size and directional consistency, with small-n caveats. A hypothesized false-stability pattern—top-k improving sliced W2 while minorities die—is not observed: at generation 1, ΔW2(top−rand)>0 on 8/8 cells (Wilcoxon p≈0.014). Directional transfer checks on 8D GMM and Digits-PCA (3 seeds each) match the same order. We release configs, seeds, logs, and analysis code with the manuscript package.

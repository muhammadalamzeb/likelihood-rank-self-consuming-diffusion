Self-consuming training loops—retraining generative models on their own outputs—can induce model collapse, including loss of rare modes. We study unlabeled selection of synthetic samples by a denoising likelihood proxy in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures. Define the minority-mass ratio r=m(1)/m(0). On a primary matrix of eight seed–imbalance cells, we report results stratified by imbalance ρ (not a single pooled test as the headline claim). At ρ=5 (n=5 seeds), generation-1 minority mass obeys bottom>rand>top on all seeds; paired retention contrasts vs random-k have large effects (top−rand: Cohen dz=−1.96, bootstrap 95% CI [−0.37,−0.15]; bottom−rand: dz=+1.65, CI [0.16,0.43]). Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank p=0.0625 for both (the minimum two-sided exact p at n=5 when all signs agree). At ρ=10 (n=3), the full order holds on 0/3 seeds and contrasts are near null. A hypothesized false-stability pattern—top-k improving sliced W2—is not observed at generation 1 (ΔW2>0 on 8/8 cells; exact Wilcoxon p=0.0078; Holm-adjusted across five generations p=0.039). Directional transfers (8D GMM, Digits-PCA; 3 seeds) match the ρ=5 order. Code: https://github.com/muhammadalamzeb/likelihood-rank-self-consuming-diffusion.

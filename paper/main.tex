\begin{abstract}
Self-consuming training loops---retraining generative models on their own outputs---can induce \emph{model collapse}, including loss of rare modes.
We study unlabeled selection of synthetic samples by a denoising likelihood proxy in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures, with emphasis on \textbf{moderate} imbalance.
Define the minority-mass ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$.
On a primary matrix of eight seed--imbalance cells, we report results \textbf{stratified by imbalance} $\rho$ (not a single pooled test as the headline claim).
At $\rho{=}5$ ($n{=}5$ seeds), generation~1 minority mass obeys $\mathrm{bottom}>\mathrm{rand}>\mathrm{top}$ on all seeds; paired retention contrasts vs.\ random-$k$ have large effects (top$-$rand: Cohen's $d_z{=}{-}1.96$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[{-}0.37,{-}0.15]$; bottom$-$rand: $d_z{=}{+}1.65$, CI $[0.16,0.43]$).
Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank $p{=}0.0625$ for both (the minimum two-sided exact $p$ achievable at $n{=}5$ when all signs agree).
At $\rho{=}10$ ($n{=}3$), the full order holds on $0/3$ seeds and contrasts are near null.
A hypothesized ``false stability'' pattern---top-$k$ improving sliced $W_2$---is not observed at generation~1 ($\Delta W_2>0$ on $8/8$ cells; exact Wilcoxon $p{=}0.0078$; Holm-adjusted across five generations $p{=}0.039$).
Directional transfers ($8$D GMM, Digits-PCA; $3$ seeds) match the $\rho{=}5$ order.
Code: \url{https://github.com/muhammadalamzeb/likelihood-rank-self-consuming-diffusion}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As synthetic data proliferates, generative models are increasingly trained on mixtures of real and model-generated samples.
Iterated self-consumption can cause \emph{model collapse}~\citep{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023,gerstgrasser2024}.
Mitigations include accumulating real data~\citep{gerstgrasser2024} and filtering synthetics~\citep{feng2024,cai2025}.
When the only score is the generator's own likelihood, how should one select synthetics?
We isolate an \emph{interventional} selection policy at matched synthetic count and matched real-mix $\alpha$, with mode-exact metrics.

\paragraph{What is new.}
A controlled top-$k$ / random-$k$ / bottom-$k$ contrast under matched budgets---not a new named training algorithm or ImageNet-scale SOTA method.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~Matched-budget DDPM protocol with exact mode masses.
(2)~Likelihood-rank ordering of minority retention, \textbf{stratified by} $\rho$.
(3)~Evidence that top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$.
(4)~Directional $8$D GMM and Digits-PCA checks.

\section{Related work}
\citet{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023} document collapse and quality-biased sampling (\emph{MAD}).
\citet{gerstgrasser2024} show accumulating real+synthetic data can avoid collapse.
\citet{feng2024} study task verification of labeled synth; \citet{cai2025} (\emph{LSF}) filter via latent probes.
Inference-time temperature methods~\citep{xu2025,li2026} reshape sampling diversity; we intervene on training-set selection.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Self-consuming loop}
Train DDPM $f^{(g)}$ on $\mathcal{D}^{(g)}$, sample synthetics, mix fixed real buffer (fraction $\alpha$) with selected synthetics.

\subsection{Likelihood proxy and policies}
Score $s(x)$: mean denoising MSE over a $t$-grid (lower $=$ higher likelihood).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{top-$k$ / bottom-$k$ / random-$k$:} pool of size $k=\max(n_{\mathrm{syn}},\lfloor k_{\mathrm{frac}}N\rfloor)$ by lowest / highest / uniform scores; then draw $n_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and mix with $\alpha$ real.
\item \textbf{mix:} same $\alpha$, but synthetic portion is a uniform subsample of all $N$ synthetics (no rank filter).
\item \textbf{replace:} pure synthetic.
\end{itemize}
Thus mix $\neq$ random-$k$.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Nearest-mean mode masses; minority-mean $m$; ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$ (``retention,'' allowed $>1$).
Sliced $W_2$: mean squared 1D Wasserstein over \textbf{24} projections; $\le$400 synth and $\le$400 held-out real points.
Hypothesis tests use the \textbf{exact} Wilcoxon signed-rank distribution over sign assignments (enumeration; midranks for magnitude ties), not only a normal approximation.

\section{Analysis}
Top-$k$ retains high-likelihood (typically majority) synthetics, reducing minority inflow.
The ordering is clear when minorities remain measurable ($\rho{=}5$) and near noise at $\rho{=}10$.
Sliced $W_2$ is a poor monitor of minority survival here.

\section{Limitations}
Toy GMMs / Digits-PCA; not ImageNet-scale.
At $\rho{=}5$, exact Wilcoxon cannot beat $p{=}0.0625$ with $n{=}5$ all-agreeing signs---inference leans on $d_z$ and unanimous direction.
$\alpha$ and $k_{\mathrm{frac}}$ ablations use two seeds; their default rows are the same experimental cell.
Transfers: $n{=}3$ cannot beat exact $p{=}0.25$.
\textbf{Multiplicity:} Holm is applied within families (two $\rho{=}5$ retention contrasts; five $W_2$ generations).
Additional stratified/exploratory tests are not multiplicity-corrected paper-wide; we treat them as descriptive under the small-sample framing.
Contribution is a controlled empirical contrast relative to MAD/LSF/Feng.

\section{Conclusion}
Likelihood ranking induces a minority-retention order that is stable at \textbf{moderate} imbalance ($\rho{=}5$) and not seed-wise reliable at $\rho{=}10$ in our matrix.
Top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1.
Preferring ``most likely'' synthetics is not a free lunch for minority survival when rare modes remain measurable.

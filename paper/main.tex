\begin{abstract}
Self-consuming training loops can induce \emph{model collapse}, including loss of rare modes.
We ask when unlabeled likelihood ranking of synthetics harms minority modes in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures.
The answer is \textbf{imbalance-dependent}: we report a moderate-imbalance boundary for the full bottom$>$rand$>$top ordering, not a universal law.
Define $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$.
At $\rho{=}5$ ($n{=}5$), generation~1 retention obeys $\mathrm{bottom}>\mathrm{rand}>\mathrm{top}$ on all seeds; bootstrap 95\% CIs for mean paired $\Delta$ vs.\ random-$k$ exclude zero (top$-$rand $[{-}0.37,{-}0.15]$; bottom$-$rand $[0.16,0.43]$), while exact Wilcoxon $p{=}0.0625$ is the discrete floor at $n{=}5$ (directional).
At intermediate $\rho{=}7$ the full order holds on only $2/5$ seeds; at $\rho{=}10$ ($n{=}8$) on $3/8$.
Top$-$rand CIs still exclude zero at $\rho{=}7$ and $\rho{=}10$, but bottom$-$rand does not at $\rho{=}10$---consistent with a partial floor on rankable minority mass.
Strongest powered claim: top-$k$ does \emph{not} improve sliced $W_2$ at generation~1 (18/18 cells positive; bootstrap CI of mean $\Delta$ excludes zero; exact $p{=}7.6{\times}10^{-6}$; Holm $p{=}3.8{\times}10^{-5}$).
Directional transfers ($8$D GMM, Digits-PCA) match $\rho{=}5$; Digits pixel-space DDPM and Digits-CVAE do not stably replicate the full order (honest negative/mixed image checks at toy resolution).
Code: \url{https://github.com/muhammadalamzeb/likelihood-rank-self-consuming-diffusion}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As synthetic data proliferates, generative models are increasingly trained on mixtures of real and model-generated samples.
Iterated self-consumption can cause \emph{model collapse}~\citep{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023,gerstgrasser2024}.
Mitigations include accumulating real data~\citep{gerstgrasser2024} and filtering synthetics~\citep{feng2024,cai2025}.
When the only score is the generator's own likelihood, how should one select synthetics?
We isolate an \emph{interventional} selection policy at matched synthetic count and matched real-mix $\alpha$, with mode-exact metrics.

\paragraph{What is new.}
An \emph{imbalance-dependent boundary}: likelihood-rank ordering of minority retention is clear at moderate imbalance and breaks down under severe imbalance---plus a falsification of ``top-$k$ looks better on $W_2$''---not a universal law or ImageNet-scale SOTA method.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~Matched-budget DDPM protocol with exact mode masses.
(2)~Stratified evidence for a moderate-imbalance regime ($\rho{=}5$) with directional bottom$>$rand$>$top ordering, vs.\ null/floor behavior at severe imbalance.
(3)~Powered evidence that top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1.
(4)~Directional $8$D GMM and Digits-PCA checks (Digits-CVAE inconclusive).

\section{Related work}
\citet{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023} document collapse and quality-biased sampling (\emph{MAD}).
\citet{gerstgrasser2024} show accumulating real+synthetic data can avoid collapse.
\citet{feng2024} study task verification of labeled synth; \citet{cai2025} (\emph{LSF}) filter via latent probes.
\citet{qiao2026} prove that selection against a \emph{biased local} real-data reference can prune global tail modes and accelerate collapse in siloed settings; their remedy uses collaborative Wasserstein proxy references.
We study a complementary failure mode: ranking synthetics by the \emph{generator's own} denoising-likelihood proxy, with a matched-budget top-$k$ / random-$k$ / bottom-$k$ contrast and minority-mass retention as the primary diversity metric.
Inference-time temperature methods~\citep{xu2025,li2026} reshape sampling diversity; we intervene on training-set selection.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Self-consuming loop}
Train DDPM $f^{(g)}$ on $\mathcal{D}^{(g)}$, sample synthetics, mix fixed real buffer (fraction $\alpha$) with selected synthetics.

\subsection{Likelihood proxy and policies}
Score $s(x)$: mean denoising MSE over a $t$-grid (lower $=$ higher likelihood).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{top-$k$ / bottom-$k$ / random-$k$:} pool of size $k=\max(n_{\mathrm{syn}},\lfloor k_{\mathrm{frac}}N\rfloor)$ by lowest / highest / uniform scores; then draw $n_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and mix with $\alpha$ real.
\item \textbf{mix:} same $\alpha$, but synthetic portion is a uniform subsample of all $N$ synthetics (no rank filter).
\item \textbf{replace:} pure synthetic.
\end{itemize}
Thus mix $\neq$ random-$k$.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Nearest-mean mode masses; minority-mean $m$; ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$ (``retention,'' allowed $>1$).
Sliced $W_2$: mean squared 1D Wasserstein over \textbf{24} projections; $\le$400 synth and $\le$400 held-out real points.
Hypothesis tests use the \textbf{exact} Wilcoxon signed-rank distribution over sign assignments (enumeration; midranks for magnitude ties), not only a normal approximation.

  top$-$rand $d_z{=}{-}2.56$, bottom$-$rand $d_z{=}{+}1.28$ (exact $p{=}0.25$).
\item \textbf{Digits-PCA:} $1.49{\pm}0.11$ / $1.11{\pm}0.08$ / $0.68{\pm}0.17$; order $3/3$;

\item \textbf{Digits pixel-space DDPM ($8{\times}8$ MLP denoiser):} order $1/3$ at $\rho{=}5$;

\section{Analysis}
Top-$k$ retains high-likelihood (typically majority) synthetics, reducing minority inflow.
The \emph{full} bottom$>$rand$>$top ordering is a moderate-imbalance phenomenon ($\rho{=}5$: $5/5$), already unreliable at $\rho{=}7$ ($2/5$) and $\rho{=}10$ ($3/8$).
Top remains worse than random on average even at higher $\rho$ (top$-$rand CIs exclude zero), but bottom's advantage fades as minority mass floors.
Sliced $W_2$ is a poor monitor of minority survival: aggregate transport can look mixed while rare-mode retention collapses under top-$k$.

\section{Limitations}
Toy GMMs and small Digits image checks; not ImageNet-scale.
At $\rho{=}5$, exact Wilcoxon cannot beat $p{=}0.0625$ with $n{=}5$---retention inference leans on bootstrap CIs and unanimous direction; the powered negative result is $W_2$ at $g{=}1$ ($n{=}18$).
$\alpha$ and $k_{\mathrm{frac}}$ ablations use two seeds; their default rows are the same experimental cell.
Transfers: $n{=}3$ cannot beat exact $p{=}0.25$; Digits-CVAE was inconclusive.
\textbf{Multiplicity:} Holm is applied within the $W_2$ generation family.
Additional stratified/exploratory tests are not multiplicity-corrected paper-wide; we treat them as descriptive under the small-sample framing and lead with CIs.
Contribution is a controlled empirical contrast complementary to MAD/LSF/Feng and to siloed-selection theory~\citep{qiao2026}; venue target is workshop / short empirical note scope.

\paragraph{Exploratory search and interpretation of $p$-values.}
The reported claim is the survivor of an earlier exploratory search over several small experimental stacks (archived under \texttt{docs/} and \texttt{experiments/} in the public repository), not a single pre-registered hypothesis tested once.
Readers should interpret retention $p$-values as descriptive under that history; we mitigate overclaiming by stratifying on $\rho$, demoting pooled tests, labeling $\rho{=}5$ full-order retention as directional, leading with bootstrap CIs, and treating the $W_2$ generation-1 result as the primary powered claim.

\section{Conclusion}
Likelihood-ranked synthetic selection induces an \textbf{imbalance-dependent} minority-retention pattern: a directional bottom$>$rand$>$top order at moderate imbalance ($\rho{=}5$), already unstable at $\rho{=}7$, and not seed-wise reliable at $\rho{=}10$.
Top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1.
Preferring ``most likely'' synthetics is not a free lunch for minority survival when rare modes remain measurable---and the effect should not be stated as a universal law.

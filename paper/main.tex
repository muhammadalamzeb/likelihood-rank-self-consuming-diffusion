\begin{abstract}
Self-consuming training loops can induce model collapse, including loss of rare modes.
We study unlabeled selection of synthetic samples by a denoising likelihood proxy in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced 2D Gaussian mixtures.
Under matched real-mix ratio and matched synthetic budget, first-generation minority-mass retention obeys
\[\mathrm{bottom}\text{-}k \;\;>\;\; \mathrm{random}\text{-}k \;\;>\;\; \mathrm{top}\text{-}k\]
(means \(0.57/0.36/0.17\) over eight seed--imbalance pairs; order holds on \(5/5\) seeds at imbalance \(\rho{=}5\)).
Paired Wilcoxon tests vs.\ random-\(k\) give \(p{\approx}0.042\) for both top and bottom contrasts.
A natural ``false stability'' hypothesis---that top-\(k\) improves sliced \(W_2\) while minorities die---is falsified.
Digits CVAE transfer was inconclusive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As synthetic data proliferates, generative models are increasingly trained on mixtures of real and generated samples.
Iterated self-consumption can cause \emph{model collapse}~\citep{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023,gerstgrasser2024}.
Mitigations include accumulating real data~\citep{gerstgrasser2024}, injecting fresh real samples, and filtering synthetics~\citep{feng2024,cai2025}.
When the only score available is the generator's own likelihood, how should one select synthetics?
We provide a matched-budget, mode-exact causal contrast among likelihood ranks.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~Controlled self-consuming DDPM protocol on imbalanced GMMs with exact mode masses.
(2)~Evidence for a likelihood-rank ordering of minority retention.
(3)~Falsification of \(W_2\)-based false stability for likelihood top-\(k\).

\section{Related work}
Model collapse and tail forgetting are documented by~\citet{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023}.
\citet{feng2024} show task verifiers for labeled synth can prevent collapse; \citet{cai2025} filter diffusion samples via latent probes (FID/precision/recall).
Inference-time temperature methods~\citep{xu2025,li2026} reshape sampling diversity; we intervene on \emph{training-set selection}.

\section{Method}
Train DDPM \(f^{(g)}\) on \(\mathcal{D}^{(g)}\), sample synthetics, mix a fixed real buffer (fraction \(\alpha\)) with selected synthetics.
Score \(s(x)\) is mean denoising MSE over a \(t\)-grid (lower $=$ higher likelihood).
Policies at matched count: top-\(k\), random-\(k\), bottom-\(k\), plus mix/replace references.
Metrics: nearest-mean mode masses and sliced \(W_2\) to held-out real data.

\section{Analysis and limitations}
Top-\(k\) retains points the model already explains well (majority modes), reducing minority inflow.
Aggregate \(W_2\) is a poor monitor for this failure.
Limitations: toy GMMs; Digits transfer inconclusive; effect strongest at moderate imbalance and early generations; L2 empirical novelty relative to MAD/LSF/Feng.

\section{Conclusion}
Unlabeled likelihood ranking induces a stable minority-retention order under matched budgets and does not deliver \(W_2\) improvements.
Preferring ``most likely'' synthetics is not a free lunch for minority survival.

\begin{abstract}
Self-consuming training loops---retraining generative models on their own outputs---can induce \emph{model collapse}, including loss of rare modes.
We study unlabeled selection of synthetic samples by a denoising likelihood proxy in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures, with emphasis on \textbf{moderate} imbalance.
Define the minority-mass ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$.
Results are \textbf{stratified by} $\rho$.
At $\rho{=}5$ ($n{=}5$), generation~1 minority mass obeys $\mathrm{bottom}>\mathrm{rand}>\mathrm{top}$ on all seeds with large paired effects vs.\ random-$k$ (top$-$rand $d_z{=}{-}1.96$; bottom$-$rand $d_z{=}{+}1.65$); we treat this as a \emph{directional} finding because exact Wilcoxon $p{=}0.0625$ is the discrete minimum at $n{=}5$ and cannot reach $p{<}0.05$.
At $\rho{=}10$ ($n{=}3$), the order fails ($0/3$) and contrasts are near null, consistent with a minority-mass floor.
Our strongest powered claim is that top-$k$ does \emph{not} improve sliced $W_2$ at generation~1 ($\Delta W_2>0$ on $8/8$ cells; exact $p{=}0.0078$; Holm $p{=}0.039$).
Directional transfers ($8$D GMM, Digits-PCA; $n{=}3$) match the $\rho{=}5$ order; a Digits-CVAE check was inconclusive.
Code: \url{https://github.com/muhammadalamzeb/likelihood-rank-self-consuming-diffusion}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As synthetic data proliferates, generative models are increasingly trained on mixtures of real and model-generated samples.
Iterated self-consumption can cause \emph{model collapse}~\citep{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023,gerstgrasser2024}.
Mitigations include accumulating real data~\citep{gerstgrasser2024} and filtering synthetics~\citep{feng2024,cai2025}.
When the only score is the generator's own likelihood, how should one select synthetics?
We isolate an \emph{interventional} selection policy at matched synthetic count and matched real-mix $\alpha$, with mode-exact metrics.

\paragraph{What is new.}
A controlled top-$k$ / random-$k$ / bottom-$k$ contrast under matched budgets, with mode-exact minority retention and an explicit falsification of ``top-$k$ looks better on $W_2$''---not a new named training algorithm or ImageNet-scale SOTA method.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~Matched-budget DDPM protocol with exact mode masses.
(2)~Directional likelihood-rank ordering of minority retention at moderate imbalance ($\rho{=}5$), \textbf{stratified by} $\rho$.
(3)~Powered evidence that top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1.
(4)~Directional $8$D GMM and Digits-PCA checks (Digits-CVAE inconclusive).

\section{Related work}
\citet{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023} document collapse and quality-biased sampling (\emph{MAD}).
\citet{gerstgrasser2024} show accumulating real+synthetic data can avoid collapse.
\citet{feng2024} study task verification of labeled synth; \citet{cai2025} (\emph{LSF}) filter via latent probes.
\citet{qiao2026} prove that selection against a \emph{biased local} real-data reference can prune global tail modes and accelerate collapse in siloed settings; their remedy uses collaborative Wasserstein proxy references.
We study a complementary failure mode: ranking synthetics by the \emph{generator's own} denoising-likelihood proxy, with a matched-budget top-$k$ / random-$k$ / bottom-$k$ contrast and minority-mass retention as the primary diversity metric.
Inference-time temperature methods~\citep{xu2025,li2026} reshape sampling diversity; we intervene on training-set selection.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Self-consuming loop}
Train DDPM $f^{(g)}$ on $\mathcal{D}^{(g)}$, sample synthetics, mix fixed real buffer (fraction $\alpha$) with selected synthetics.

\subsection{Likelihood proxy and policies}
Score $s(x)$: mean denoising MSE over a $t$-grid (lower $=$ higher likelihood).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{top-$k$ / bottom-$k$ / random-$k$:} pool of size $k=\max(n_{\mathrm{syn}},\lfloor k_{\mathrm{frac}}N\rfloor)$ by lowest / highest / uniform scores; then draw $n_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and mix with $\alpha$ real.
\item \textbf{mix:} same $\alpha$, but synthetic portion is a uniform subsample of all $N$ synthetics (no rank filter).
\item \textbf{replace:} pure synthetic.
\end{itemize}
Thus mix $\neq$ random-$k$.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Nearest-mean mode masses; minority-mean $m$; ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$ (``retention,'' allowed $>1$).
Sliced $W_2$: mean squared 1D Wasserstein over \textbf{24} projections; $\le$400 synth and $\le$400 held-out real points.
Hypothesis tests use the \textbf{exact} Wilcoxon signed-rank distribution over sign assignments (enumeration; midranks for magnitude ties), not only a normal approximation.

At $\rho{=}5$, all five seeds agree in sign with large effects ($|d_z|{>}1.6$).
Exact two-sided $p{=}0.0625$ is the smallest attainable at $n{=}5$ under the signed-rank null when all signs agree; the test \emph{cannot} reject at $\alpha{=}0.05$ no matter how large the effect.
We therefore report the $\rho{=}5$ ordering as \textbf{directional / suggestive} (unanimous signs + effect sizes + CIs excluding zero for the mean $\Delta$), not as a claim that clears a conventional significance threshold.
At $\rho{=}10$, minorities are already near floor at $g{=}0$: few minority synthetics remain to rank, so top/random/bottom pools become statistically similar and contrasts are consistent with null (a saturation / floor hypothesis; isolating equal-minority-count designs is left to future work).
For completeness, a pooled $n{=}8$ exact Wilcoxon yields $p{=}0.039$ for both contrasts, but we treat that as secondary because it mixes heterogeneous strata.

\subsection{Transfer checks (directional; $n{=}3$)}
Minimum two-sided exact Wilcoxon $p$ at $n{=}3$ with all signs agreeing is $0.25$; we report Cohen's $d_z$ against that bound and do not claim powered generalization.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$8$D GMM:} $0.55{\pm}0.20$ / $0.27{\pm}0.06$ / $0.08{\pm}0.03$; order $3/3$;
  top$-$rand $d_z{=}{-}2.56$, bottom$-$rand $d_z{=}{+}1.28$ (exact $p{=}0.25$).
\item \textbf{Digits-PCA:} $1.49{\pm}0.11$ / $1.11{\pm}0.08$ / $0.68{\pm}0.17$; order $3/3$;
  top$-$rand $d_z{=}{-}1.72$, bottom$-$rand $d_z{=}{+}2.67$ (exact $p{=}0.25$).
\item \textbf{Digits-CVAE (inconclusive):} a pixel-space conditional VAE transfer did not reproduce a stable bottom$>$rand$>$top order under the same budget protocol; we treat Digits-PCA (mode masses in PCA space) as the supported image-adjacent check and do not claim CVAE replication.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis}
Top-$k$ retains high-likelihood (typically majority) synthetics, reducing minority inflow.
The ordering is directionally clear when minorities remain measurable ($\rho{=}5$) and near noise at $\rho{=}10$, consistent with a floor on rankable minority mass.
Sliced $W_2$ is a poor monitor of minority survival here: aggregate transport can look acceptable while rare-mode retention collapses.

\section{Limitations}
Toy GMMs / Digits-PCA; not ImageNet-scale.
At $\rho{=}5$, exact Wilcoxon cannot beat $p{=}0.0625$ with $n{=}5$ all-agreeing signs---retention inference is directional ($d_z$, unanimous signs); the powered negative result is the $W_2$ contrast at $g{=}1$.
$\alpha$ and $k_{\mathrm{frac}}$ ablations use two seeds; their default rows are the same experimental cell.
Transfers: $n{=}3$ cannot beat exact $p{=}0.25$; Digits-CVAE was inconclusive.
\textbf{Multiplicity:} Holm is applied within families (two $\rho{=}5$ retention contrasts; five $W_2$ generations).
Additional stratified/exploratory tests are not multiplicity-corrected paper-wide; we treat them as descriptive under the small-sample framing.
Contribution is a controlled empirical contrast complementary to MAD/LSF/Feng and to siloed-selection theory~\citep{qiao2026}.

\paragraph{Exploratory search and interpretation of $p$-values.}
The reported claim is the survivor of an earlier exploratory search over several small experimental stacks (archived under \texttt{docs/} and \texttt{experiments/} in the public repository), not a single pre-registered hypothesis tested once.
Readers should interpret retention $p$-values as descriptive under that history; we mitigate overclaiming by stratifying on $\rho$, demoting pooled tests, labeling $\rho{=}5$ retention as directional, and treating the $W_2$ generation-1 result as the primary powered claim.

\section{Conclusion}
At moderate imbalance ($\rho{=}5$), likelihood ranking yields a directional minority-retention order $\mathrm{bottom}>\mathrm{rand}>\mathrm{top}$; the order is not seed-wise reliable at $\rho{=}10$ in our matrix, consistent with a minority-mass floor.
Top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1.
Preferring ``most likely'' synthetics is not a free lunch for minority survival when rare modes remain measurable.

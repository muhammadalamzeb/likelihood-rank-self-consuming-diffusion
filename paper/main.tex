\begin{abstract}
Self-consuming training loops can induce \emph{model collapse}, including loss of rare modes.
We study unlabeled likelihood ranking of synthetics in self-consuming DDPM training on imbalanced mixtures.
\textbf{Primary claim:} selecting by top likelihood does \emph{not} improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1
($\Delta W_2>0$ on $23/23$ seed--imbalance cells; bootstrap 95\% CI of the mean excludes zero; Wilcoxon $p{=}2.9{\times}10^{-5}$, Holm $p{=}1.4{\times}10^{-4}$).
\textbf{Secondary, post-hoc / narrow-regime observation:} after an original ``false stability'' hypothesis was falsified, we report that full bottom$>$rand$>$top minority retention appears only at moderate imbalance $\rho{=}5$ ($n{=}10$, $10/10$; exact Wilcoxon $p{=}0.002$; CIs exclude zero), and already fails for most seeds at $\rho{=}7$ ($2/5$) and $\rho{=}10$ ($3/8$).
Treat that ordering as exploratory (elevated HARKing risk), not a pre-registered law.
Workshop-scale note on controllable mixtures and tiny Digits checks---\emph{not} ImageNet-scale diffusion.
Code: \url{https://github.com/muhammadalamzeb/likelihood-rank-self-consuming-diffusion}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
As synthetic data proliferates, generative models are increasingly trained on mixtures of real and model-generated samples.
Iterated self-consumption can cause \emph{model collapse}~\citep{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023,gerstgrasser2024}.
Mitigations include accumulating real data~\citep{gerstgrasser2024} and filtering synthetics~\citep{feng2024,cai2025}.
When the only score is the generator's own likelihood, how should one select synthetics?
We isolate an \emph{interventional} selection policy at matched synthetic count and matched real-mix $\alpha$, with mode-exact metrics.

\paragraph{What is new.}
A powered falsification of ``top-$k$ looks better on aggregate $W_2$,'' plus a \emph{narrow-regime} minority-retention observation at moderate imbalance that does \emph{not} generalize to higher $\rho$ or to image-scale models.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~Matched-budget DDPM protocol with exact mode masses.
(2)~Primary: top-$k$ does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1 ($n{=}23$ cells).
(3)~Secondary: bottom$>$rand$>$top minority retention at $\rho{=}5$ ($n{=}10$), unreliable at $\rho{=}7,10$.
(4)~Tiny Digits checks (PCA supports; pixel DDPM / CVAE do not stably replicate the full order).

\section{Related work}
\citet{shumailov2023,alemohammad2023} document collapse and quality-biased sampling (\emph{MAD}).
\citet{gerstgrasser2024} show accumulating real+synthetic data can avoid collapse.
\citet{feng2024} study task verification of labeled synth; \citet{cai2025} (\emph{LSF}) filter via latent probes.
\citet{qiao2026} prove that selection against a \emph{biased local} real-data reference can prune global tail modes and accelerate collapse in siloed settings; their remedy uses collaborative Wasserstein proxy references.
We study a complementary failure mode: ranking synthetics by the \emph{generator's own} denoising-likelihood proxy, with a matched-budget top-$k$ / random-$k$ / bottom-$k$ contrast and minority-mass retention as the primary diversity metric.
Inference-time temperature methods~\citep{xu2025,li2026} reshape sampling diversity; we intervene on training-set selection.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Self-consuming loop}
Train DDPM $f^{(g)}$ on $\mathcal{D}^{(g)}$, sample synthetics, mix fixed real buffer (fraction $\alpha$) with selected synthetics.

\subsection{Likelihood proxy and policies}
Score $s(x)$: mean denoising MSE over a $t$-grid (lower $=$ higher likelihood).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{top-$k$ / bottom-$k$ / random-$k$:} pool of size $k=\max(n_{\mathrm{syn}},\lfloor k_{\mathrm{frac}}N\rfloor)$ by lowest / highest / uniform scores; then draw $n_{\mathrm{syn}}$ and mix with $\alpha$ real.
\item \textbf{mix:} same $\alpha$, but synthetic portion is a uniform subsample of all $N$ synthetics (no rank filter).
\item \textbf{replace:} pure synthetic.
\end{itemize}
Thus mix $\neq$ random-$k$.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Nearest-mean mode masses; minority-mean $m$; ratio $r=m^{(1)}/m^{(0)}$ (``retention,'' allowed $>1$).
Sliced $W_2$: mean squared 1D Wasserstein over \textbf{24} projections; $\le$400 synth and $\le$400 held-out real points.
Hypothesis tests: bootstrap 95\% CIs of the mean paired $\Delta$; Wilcoxon signed-rank with \textbf{exact} sign enumeration for $n{\le}16$ (midranks for ties) and a normal approximation for larger $n$.

\section{Analysis}
Top-$k$ retains high-likelihood (typically majority) synthetics.
Aggregate sliced $W_2$ is a poor monitor of minority survival: top-$k$ is consistently \emph{worse} on $W_2$ at $g{=}1$ across the full matrix, while minority retention shows a \emph{narrow} bottom$>$rand$>$top regime only at $\rho{=}5$.
That regime already fails for most seeds at $\rho{=}7$ and $\rho{=}10$.

\section{Limitations}
\textbf{Scale.} All primary results are on 2D/8D GMMs and tiny Digits ($8{\times}8$) checks.
We have \emph{not} demonstrated these effects on any real image-scale diffusion model (e.g.\ CIFAR/ImageNet UNet or DiT); Digits pixel-space DDPM does not stably replicate the full retention order ($1/3$).
\textbf{Scope.} Workshop / short empirical note---not a definitive scaling result.
$\alpha$ and $k_{\mathrm{frac}}$ ablations use two seeds; their default rows are the same experimental cell.
\textbf{Multiplicity:} Holm within the $W_2$ generation family and within the two $\rho{=}5$ retention contrasts; other tests descriptive.
Complementary to MAD/LSF/Feng and siloed-selection theory~\citep{qiao2026}.

\paragraph{Researcher degrees of freedom and post-hoc revision (important).}
This note is the survivor of a long, documented exploratory search across many unrelated directions before self-consuming diffusion (archived under \texttt{docs/} in the public repository).
Within this topic, the \emph{original} working hypothesis was a ``false stability'' dissociation: top-$k$ would \emph{improve} sliced $W_2$ while secretly accelerating minority collapse.
The pilot \textbf{falsified} that dissociation (top-$k$ worsened $W_2$), after which the claim was revised to the present imbalance-stratified retention ordering plus an explicit $W_2$ negative result.
Readers should therefore treat the bottom$>$rand$>$top ordering as a \textbf{post-hoc / exploratory} finding with elevated HARKing risk---even though $\rho{=}5$ now uses $n{=}10$ seeds and exact $p{=}0.002$---and should \textbf{not} read it as a pre-registered general law.
The $W_2$ negative result is comparatively more trustworthy: it was the observation that killed the original hypothesis, then held on the expanded $n{=}23$ cell matrix with unanimous sign at $g{=}1$.
We mitigate overclaiming by making $W_2$ primary in the abstract, stratifying retention by $\rho$, demoting pooled tests, and leading with bootstrap CIs.

\section{Conclusion}
Top-$k$ likelihood selection does not improve sliced $W_2$ vs.\ random-$k$ at generation~1 in our matrix.
A bottom$>$rand$>$top minority-retention order appears in a \textbf{narrow} moderate-imbalance regime ($\rho{=}5$) and should not be stated as a general law.
Preferring ``most likely'' synthetics is not a free lunch on either aggregate $W_2$ or rare-mode survival when minorities remain measurable.
